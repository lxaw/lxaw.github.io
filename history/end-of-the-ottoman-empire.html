The End of the Ottoman Empire

1918 – 1923 · see on the map

The Ottoman Empire did not fall in a single moment. Between the armistice of October 1918 and the treaty of July 1923, it lost a war, was occupied, was partitioned on paper, and was then replaced from within by a rival government in Ankara that fought its way to a different settlement.

Armistice of Mudros 30 October 1918

The empire left the First World War with an armistice signed aboard HMS Agamemnon in Mudros harbour, on the island of Lemnos. It opened the Straits to the Allies and let them occupy any strategic point they judged necessary to their security.

Occupation of Constantinople from 13 November 1918

Allied forces entered the capital two weeks later. The occupation became formal on 16 March 1920 and lasted until the last Allied troops left on 4 October 1923.

Greek landing at Smyrna 15 May 1919

Greek troops landed at Smyrna with Allied backing. The landing began the Greco-Turkish War and did more than any other single event to turn scattered resistance in Anatolia into a movement.

Samsun 19 May 1919

Four days later Mustafa Kemal arrived at Samsun on the Black Sea coast, sent as an army inspector. He instead organised the resistance, through congresses at Erzurum and Sivas that summer and then, on 23 April 1920, a Grand National Assembly in Ankara that claimed to speak for the nation in place of the occupied capital.

Treaty of Sèvres 10 August 1920

The sultan's government signed a peace that reduced the empire to part of Anatolia, with zones for Greece, France and Italy and provision for an Armenian state and a possible Kurdish one. It was never ratified, and the Ankara government rejected it outright.

Battle of the Sakarya 23 August – 13 September 1921

The Greek army advanced to within reach of Ankara and was stopped along the Sakarya River after three weeks of fighting. It was the furthest the advance got.

Dumlupınar 26 – 30 August 1922

A year later the Turkish Great Offensive broke the Greek lines, with the decisive battle at Dumlupınar on 30 August. Turkish forces entered Smyrna on 9 September.

Armistice of Mudanya 11 October 1922

With Turkish and British troops facing each other at the Straits, an armistice signed at Mudanya conceded Eastern Thrace and, in time, Constantinople to the Ankara government.

Abolition of the sultanate 1 November 1922

The Grand National Assembly abolished the sultanate, ending a dynasty that had ruled for roughly six centuries. Mehmed VI left Constantinople on 17 November aboard the British battleship HMS Malaya. His cousin Abdülmecid II kept the title of caliph until that office was abolished too, on 3 March 1924.

Treaty of Lausanne 24 July 1923

Lausanne replaced Sèvres and fixed most of the borders of the new state. The Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923, with Ankara as its capital.
